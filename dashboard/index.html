The 10 monitored channels

Who runs them, what they're good for, and how to read them. Subscriber counts verified 2 July 2026.

Рыбарь · Rybar

@rybar · 1.55M subscribers

Run by Mikhail Zvinchuk, former Defence Ministry press-service officer, with a staff of ~40. Detailed maps, structured daily chronicles, long-form analysis.

How to read: the most "institutional" channel — closest proxy for MoD-adjacent thinking; watch it for doctrine signals and campaign framing.

Два майора · Two Majors

@dva_majors · 1.16M subscribers

Anonymous collective; among the most prominent Z-channels. High-volume aggregation: regional strike reports, enemy-source monitoring, heavy fundraising.

How to read: fast and broad; useful for volume trends and relayed Ukrainian claims, but lots of noise to filter.

WarGonzo

@wargonzo · 754K subscribers

Semyon Pegov — among the first bloggers accredited by the MoD, decorated by Putin. Frontline reportage plus a daily morning frontline summary by direction.

How to read: the morning svodka is a compact map of claimed frontline changes; reportage shows what access the MoD grants.

Сладков+ · Sladkov+

@Sladkov_plus · 707K subscribers

Aleksandr Sladkov, veteran VGTRK state-broadcaster war correspondent.

How to read: mainline state-TV voice; deviation from official framing here is itself a signal.

Поддубный |Z|O|V| · Poddubny

@epoddubny · 578K subscribers

Yevgeny Poddubny, Rossiya-1 correspondent, named Hero of Russia in 2024.

How to read: as close to official as the milblogger space gets; useful as the "baseline" narrative.

Methodology

How the daily brief is produced

1. Collection. Every morning at 06:00 UTC the system pulls the previous 24 hours of posts from all 10 channels via the Telegram API. Raw posts (original Russian) are archived permanently before any processing.

2. Filtering. Ads, fundraisers, merchandise, memes and personal notes are removed. Typically ~40% of posts survive as "substantive".

3. Classification. Claude (Anthropic's AI model) translates each post and assigns one topic and one subtopic. The Criticism topic captures posts whose main point is complaint or blame, with subcategories for the target: army command & logistics, government & ministries, Putin personally, regional authorities.

4. Sentiment. Each post is classified negative, neutral or positive — the blogger's own mood about the subject, from Russia's perspective (e.g. a post celebrating a strike = positive; a post complaining about drone attacks on Crimea = negative). Topic-level scores are simple shares of posts; net balance = % positive − % negative. Criticism posts are negative by definition, so that topic is tracked by volume and target instead.

5. Flags. A story is flagged NEW when a theme absent from the previous 14 days appears in ≥2 channels; MOOD SWING when a topic's net balance moves ≥15 points day-on-day; and a standing watchlist (mobilisation rumours, criticism of Putin, nuclear signalling, Belarus entry) is checked every day.

6. Corroboration tags. ✓ = claim repeated independently by 2+ channels; ◌ = single source. Repetition across propaganda channels is not verification — it measures how hard a message is being pushed.

Limitations. All source material is biased; sentiment reflects the bloggers' mood, not battlefield reality; channel-relayed "Ukrainian sources" are selectively quoted; counts depend on Telegram API availability.
