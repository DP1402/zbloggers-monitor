The 10 monitored channels

Who runs them, what they're good for, and how to read them. Subscriber counts verified 2 July 2026.

Рыбарь · Rybar

@rybar · 1.55M subscribers

Run by Mikhail Zvinchuk, former Defence Ministry press-service officer, with a staff of ~40. Detailed maps, structured daily chronicles, long-form analysis.

How to read: the most "institutional" channel — closest proxy for MoD-adjacent thinking; watch it for doctrine signals and campaign framing.

Два майора · Two Majors

@dva_majors · 1.16M subscribers

Anonymous collective; among the most prominent Z-channels. High-volume aggregation: regional strike reports, enemy-source monitoring, heavy fundraising.

How to read: fast and broad; useful for volume trends and relayed Ukrainian claims, but lots of noise to filter.

WarGonzo

@wargonzo · 754K subscribers

Semyon Pegov — among the first bloggers accredited by the MoD, decorated by Putin. Frontline reportage plus a daily morning frontline summary by direction.

How to read: the morning svodka is a compact map of claimed frontline changes; reportage shows what access the MoD grants.

Сладков+ · Sladkov+

@Sladkov_plus · 707K subscribers

Aleksandr Sladkov, veteran VGTRK state-broadcaster war correspondent.

How to read: mainline state-TV voice; deviation from official framing here is itself a signal.

Поддубный |Z|O|V| · Poddubny

@epoddubny · 578K subscribers

Yevgeny Poddubny, Rossiya-1 correspondent, named Hero of Russia in 2024.

How to read: as close to official as the milblogger space gets; useful as the "baseline" narrative.

Kotsnews

@sashakots · 479K subscribers

Aleksandr Kots of Komsomolskaya Pravda; member of the presidential Human Rights Council. Known for detailed strike "razbory" (breakdowns).

How to read: his target-by-target strike analyses are the most specific claims available — ideal for cross-checking against damage reports.

GREY ZONE

@grey_zone · 343K subscribers

Wagner-affiliated, anonymous. Harder edge; historically willing to criticise the MoD. Dormant since July 2025 — kept as a tripwire: a revival would itself be a signal.

How to read: a barometer of siloviki-adjacent discontent; criticism here often precedes wider milblogger frustration.

Архангел Спецназа · Arkhangel Spetsnaza

@rusich_army · 1.04M subscribers

Anonymous hardline channel linked to VDV circles (investigated by iStories, Sep 2024); graphic content, aggressive tone, big fundraising operation.

How to read: the hardline id of the movement; watch for what it demands (escalation, mobilisation) rather than what it reports.

Старше Эдды · Starshe Eddy

@vysokygovorit · 491K subscribers

Prominent ideological/nationalist commentary; author self-describes as a deputy working in a Saratov constituency.

How to read: best single source for the ideological mood — rearmament anxiety, civilisational framing, morale essays.

НгП раZVедка · NgP raZVedka

@ngp_razvedka · 96K subscribers

Smaller channel known for aggressive anti-NATO framing; widely cited by peers.

How to read: rumour incubator — claims often surface here before larger channels pick them up.

Methodology

How the daily brief is produced

Sources & bias. ⚠️ All monitored channels are pro-Kremlin propaganda outlets. Claims are signals to investigate, not facts. Sentiment reflects the bloggers' mood, not battlefield reality; channel-relayed "Ukrainian sources" are selectively quoted.

1. Collection. Every morning at ~05:30 UTC the system pulls the previous day's posts from all 10 channels via the Telegram API, including views and forward counts. Raw posts (original Russian) are archived permanently in monthly CSV files before any processing.

2. Filtering. Ads, fundraisers, merchandise, memes and personal notes are removed by the classifier. Typically ~40% of posts survive as "substantive".

3. Classification. Claude (Anthropic's AI) translates each post and assigns one topic and one subtopic from a fixed taxonomy. The Criticism topic captures posts whose main point is complaint or blame, with subcategories for the target: army command & logistics, government & ministries, Putin personally, regional authorities.

4. Sentiment. Each post is classified negative, neutral or positive — the blogger's own mood about the subject, from Russia's perspective. Topic scores are shares of posts; net balance = % positive − % negative. Criticism posts are negative by definition, so that topic is tracked by volume and target instead.

5. Flags. A story is flagged NEW when a subtopic absent from the previous 14 days draws 3+ posts in 2+ channels; MOOD SWING when a topic's net balance moves ≥25 points day-on-day; and a standing watchlist (mobilisation rumours, criticism of Putin) alerts the moment 2+ channels touch it.

6. Brief. A stronger Claude model writes the daily brief, narrative watch and mood summary from the classified posts, with a link to every source post. ✓ = claim repeated by 2+ channels; ◌ = single source. Repetition across propaganda channels is not verification — it measures how hard a message is being pushed.

Data access. Raw posts: data/posts/YYYY-MM.csv. Classifications: data/analysis/classified-YYYY-MM.csv. Daily analysis: data/analysis/days/. All in the project's GitHub repository.

Limitations. Counts depend on Telegram API availability; engagement figures are Telegram's own and can be inflated; classification and sentiment are automated and imperfect — click through to source posts before relying on any specific claim.
